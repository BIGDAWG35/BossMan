# LAETT / IBEW 11 — Public Information Guide

> **Status:** Informational only. Not legal, financial, or career advice.
> This document is a public summary of the Los Angeles Electrical Training Trust (LAETT / JATC) and IBEW Local 11, assembled from the organizations' own public websites, the California Division of Apprenticeship Standards (DAS), the U.S. Department of Labor Office of Apprenticeship, and the Better Business Bureau. Verify all details with the source institutions before acting.

## The two institutions at a glance

| Institution | What it is | Public website |
|---|---|---|
| **IBEW Local 11** ("the union") | International Brotherhood of Electrical Workers, local union covering Los Angeles and surrounding counties. Represents roughly 12,000+ commercial, industrial, and residential electricians. | ibew11.org |
| **LAETT** (Los Angeles Electrical Training Trust / "the JATC") | Joint Apprenticeship & Training Committee — a 50/50 labor–management trust that runs the apprenticeship, training, and continuing-education programs for IBEW Local 11 members. Operates the training center at 6023 Garfield Ave., Commerce, CA 90040. | laett.com |

The two are separate legal entities. Joining IBEW Local 11 as a journeyman is a separate process from applying to the apprenticeship.

## The Inside Wireman apprenticeship (the flagship program)

- **Length:** Five years (10 semesters).
- **Structure:** 800+ hours of related supplemental instruction (RSI / classroom) plus 8,000+ hours of on-the-job training (OJT) under the direction of a journeyman.
- **Pay model:** Apprentices are paid wages from day one, scaled to a percentage of the journeyman wage and stepped up each period (typically six-month periods, six steps).
- **Class schedule:** RSI is generally two evenings per week plus some Saturdays, totaling about 160 hours per year. The mix of evening classes lets apprentices work full-time during the day.
- **Application window:** Applications are accepted online through the LAETT website. The window is typically a few weeks long; check the LAETT site for the exact dates of the next cycle. **Do not pay a third party to apply — applications are free through the official site.**
- **Minimum qualifications at the time of application:** 18 years old, high school diploma or equivalent (sealed transcripts required), one year of high-school algebra with a grade of C or better, valid California driver's license, and proof of U.S. work authorization. A passing score on the LAETT aptitude test is required before indenture.
- **What the application asks for:** Education history, employment history, and a list of people who are not relatives for references. Background and driving checks come later in the process.
- **Cost to apply:** $0 through the official channel.

### What the apprenticeship leads to

- **Journeyman Inside Wireman** status after five years and successful completion of all RSI/OJT requirements.
- Industry-recognized credentials issued by the program, including OSHA-10 (or OSHA-30 in some tracks), CPR / first aid, and others required by signatory contractors.
- Eligibility for IBEW Local 11 membership as a journeyman.

## How the program is funded

The JATC is a 501(c)(3) trust. The trust is funded by contributions from signatory electrical contractors and from IBEW Local 11 itself, governed by a board that is half contractor-side and half union-side. EIN 95-6123788 (publicly listed on GuideStar / ProPublica Nonprofit Explorer). The trust's purpose is training — not profit — and apprentice tuition is paid for by the trust, not by the apprentice.

---

## Other programs at LAETT (public summary)

In addition to the five-year Inside Wireman apprenticeship, LAETT runs continuing-education and journeyman-upgrade courses for IBEW 11 members and, in some cases, non-member electricians who need specific certifications. The catalog rotates; the current class schedule and fees are published on the LAETT website. A separate Sound & Communications installer track and a Transportation Electrician track are also offered under the same JATC umbrella.

## The application process — public-domain overview

1. **Watch the application window.** Sign up on the LAETT site for notifications; applications open a few times per year.
2. **Submit the online application.** Free, direct, and the only legitimate channel. Be cautious of any third-party service that offers to "help you apply" for a fee.
3. **Take the aptitude test.** A timed math and reading-comprehension test administered at the training center.
4. **Interview.** Selected applicants are invited to an in-person interview at the training center.
5. **Conditional offer / indenture.** Successful applicants are offered an indenture, complete a background check and a drug screen, and are matched with a signatory contractor for OJT.

The full process from application to first day of OJT is typically several months. Plan for the timeline.

## California legal framework (summary, public domain)

California's apprenticeship system is governed by the **Shelly-Berman California Apprenticeship Council** within the Division of Apprenticeship Standards (DAS), in the Department of Industrial Relations. State law (Labor Code §§ 3070–3098) requires every apprenticeable occupation to be administered by an apprenticeship committee approved by DAS. The full text of these statutes is published on the California Legislative Information website (leginfo.legislature.ca.gov) and mirrored on Justia.com. **Always confirm with a current source — this summary is informational.**

## Common questions (public-domain answers)

**Q: Do I need to know someone in the union to apply?**
A: No. Applications are open to anyone who meets the minimum qualifications. Selection is by application, test, and interview.

**Q: How much does the apprenticeship cost?**
A: The RSI (classroom) portion is paid for by the trust. Apprentices are paid wages during OJT.

**Q: Is this a union-only program?**
A: It is jointly run by the union and signatory contractors. Graduates become IBEW Local 11 members.

**Q: Can I apply if I'm not yet 18?**
A: No. You must be 18 at the time of indenture.

**Q: What if I don't live in Los Angeles?**
A: IBEW Local 11 covers a multi-county Southern California area. Other parts of California are covered by other IBEW locals (for example, IBEW 6 in Northern California, IBEW 47 in San Diego, IBEW 465 in San Diego County, etc.). Out-of-state applicants would generally relocate.

**Q: Can women apply?**
A: Yes. The program is open to all qualified applicants. LAETT participates in outreach efforts aimed at increasing female participation in the trades.
